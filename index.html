Discover Engine

See how a business appears across public sources.

Discover Engine checks selected public information, compares what the sources show and organizes the result into a clear record you can trace back to its source.

Look up a business

What it helps you see

One business can look different depending on where you look.

A search may find information that matches across sources, contact details that differ, older public material, public technical signals or details that still need review. Not every search returns every kind of observation.

What lines up

See when multiple checked sources report the same or closely matching information.

What differs

Notice conflicting details or older material without quietly choosing one version as correct.

What needs review

Keep unclear information visible, with enough context to decide what deserves another look.

The Case File

A clear record of what the checked sources reported.

The Case File brings the observations for one business into a structured summary. It keeps useful context with the result, including the source, timing and how strongly the information lines up across the checked sources.

It is designed to make scattered evidence easier to read and compare—not to replace direct confirmation with the business.

Business research is the first use

A reusable approach to public evidence.

Discover Engine begins with business research, but its broader purpose is to help organize and compare public evidence around other focused questions over time.

A careful view

What the sources show, with the limits left visible.

Discover Engine shows where information came from and where public sources agree or disagree. Agreement can add context, but it is not independent proof that every detail is correct.

Public information only

The lookup does not access private accounts and does not claim to cover the whole internet. It reports what its selected public checks found and marks unclear information for review.

Waypoint

Context that changes when life changes.

A functional prototype that turns a reported life change into an updated situation record, possible cross-domain effects and one prioritized next step.

A working architectural demonstration

An evolving picture of a person’s situation.

Waypoint accepts a change in plain language, updates the relevant life category, preserves the prior value in a change history and explains why one suggested action comes first.

The prototype demonstrates a general life-transition architecture. VetBridge, neurodivergent support and other specialized applications remain concepts rather than operational products.

Current boundary

State persists only in the user’s current browser. Updates are applied without a confirmation gate, and no crisis safeguards, professional-advice support, accounts, cloud sync, voice input, passive sensing or NeuroEdge integration are implemented.

Thought Ledger

Turn scattered thoughts into connected knowledge.

A persistent knowledge workspace for ideas that develop over time—across notes, research, documents, decisions and conversations.

A place for evolving context

Not another pile of disconnected notes.

Thought Ledger captures raw material and organizes it into topic-based Threads, where context can accumulate instead of being lost across separate tools and sessions.

Its AI capabilities can sort captures, answer from a Thread’s accumulated context, check a related Thread when relevant and search the web when current outside information is needed. AI supports the knowledge workspace; it is not the entire purpose of the product.

NeuroEdge

Sense change. Process locally. Preserve only what matters.

A proposed neuromorphic edge architecture for converting physical change into sparse events, processing those events locally and minimizing unnecessary raw-data movement.

Architecture

A local event-processing path.

The proposed architecture converts physical change into a sparse event stream, processes it locally and retains only the result required by the application.

Hybrid frame-event sensor

Physical changes are captured through frame and event signals.

Encoding and filtering

Relevant changes are filtered and converted into sparse events.

Event stream

An AER-equivalent representation carries events without reconstructing continuous frames.

Neuromorphic processing

An NPU or spiking neural network processes the event stream locally.

Local classification

The system produces the application-relevant result at the edge.

Data destruction

Raw or intermediate data can be discarded after the required result is produced.

Why local processing matters

Less data movement. Faster response. Clearer privacy boundaries.

Automotive and fleet safety

Explore rapid local interpretation of relevant physical events without continuous cloud dependence.

Healthcare and biometric sensing

Explore privacy-conscious local classification where transmitting raw sensing data may be undesirable.

Verification and anti-spoofing

Explore event signatures that help a device distinguish meaningful live change from static or replayed input.

These are research and application directions. They are not claims of a manufactured, clinically validated or deployed product.

About TFI

An independent systems-development company.

Thompson Foundry Innovations LLC is a California company developing original systems, architectures and research around difficult information and sensing problems.

The company

Systems before standalone products.

TFI does not begin by forcing every problem into an app. It investigates the underlying structure, develops reusable capabilities and then considers which applications, partnerships or deployments make sense.

The current portfolio spans evidence intelligence, contextual assistance, personal knowledge organization, study-system architecture and neuromorphic edge sensing.
