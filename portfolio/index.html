Discover Engine: one engine, many applications

A proprietary engine that turns scattered evidence into structured, connected knowledge.
2 APPS · 1 CORE
The problem

Very different problems share the same hard part: finding the evidence, pulling out the facts, settling conflicts between sources, and showing exactly where every fact came from.

What I built
  • One core pipeline: Evidence, Capture, Extraction, Findings, Resolution, Unified Profile.
  • Swappable domain packs. The engine is the player and each pack is a tape it plays, so the same core can serve a local business, a defense trade study or a records system.
  • Trust rules built in: every fact tied to its source, labeled as observed, estimated or confirmed, and AI-only findings stop at "Likely" until a person confirms them.
The result

TFI Signals runs on the business pack and Titrant runs on a defense decision pack. Version 6 combines what both proved, and new packs are being designed for government records and decision work.

Guiding principle: to understand what doesn't exist, you first have to know what does.

02

Titrant: defense trade studies you can trace

Every decision tied to its criteria, assumptions, bias checks and evidence.
ARMY SBIR SUBMITTED
The problem

Trade studies compare alternatives for big decisions, but the reasons behind a choice often hide in assumptions nobody wrote down and biases nobody checked. Reviewers can't trace why one option won.

What I built
  • A governed workflow: criteria, constraints, gating assumptions, bias checks and evidence for each alternative.
  • Code does the scoring, not AI. AI only drafts items, a person approves every one, and the audit trail records who approved what and when.
  • A sensitivity chart that shows exactly where the winner would change, and a plain "why this option wins" summary linked to the evidence.
  • A signer-ready decision package that exports to PDF.
  • I tested it as a first-time user, found where people got lost, and added a step-by-step guide and clearer review screens.
The result

In the fictional silent-watch power demo, Alternative C won 0.60 to 0.50 to 0.40, and the smallest change that flips the winner is raising the weight on vehicle weight from 0.20 to 0.33. Titrant was submitted as the prototype for an Army SBIR proposal in October 2026, and its evidence pipeline became the base for Discover Engine version 6.

The Silicon Brain: three linked inventions

Brain-inspired sensing that sees, judges what matters and learns from what happens.
22 TESTS LOGGED
The idea
  • NeuroEdge, the eyes: an event-based edge sensor system that processes on the device and deletes raw data after each cycle. USPTO provisional patent 64/070,641.
  • Silicon Amygdala, the gut check: decides which movements matter and when to raise a flag, and says why.
  • Silicon Frontal Cortex, the thinking part: takes a second look at flags, learns from how they turned out, and helps the system stop repeating false alarms.
How I work

Every experiment is planned in writing before it runs, run twice, and kept on record, failures included. The system measures facial movements using the FACS standard and makes no claim to read emotions.

Results so far (simulation only)
  • Noise filter cut false alarms 45 to 61%, and held on fresh data three times.
  • With 10% of events missing, it missed 0 of 400 real signals. The earlier design missed 214.
  • The Amygdala caught 79 to 85% of unusual episodes, and the reason it gave was right 95 to 99% of the time.
  • The first Frontal Cortex test failed its own pass marks. That failure is on record and shaped the next test.
What's next

First real use in my own workspace with a webcam, then real camera data.

Waypoint: help before the crisis

A guide that remembers your situation and walks you to the next right step.
LIVE · IN BETA
The problem

A job loss, leaving the military, release from incarceration, a new diagnosis, a rent increase. The help usually exists, but it's scattered across dozens of places, and it tends to arrive after the crisis instead of at the turning point.

Why I built it

I lived this problem. I went years without help I qualified for because no one connected the dots. Waypoint is the guide I never had.

What I built
  • Each situation is an open storyline with its history, contacts, dates and documents, kept until the person says it's done.
  • It does the work: benefit estimates, real form filling, drafted messages and referrals that follow up until a program answers.
  • English and Spanish, voice, an easier-reading mode, and a read-only link for a trusted helper.
  • Nothing changes without the person's yes, and ID numbers are removed before the AI sees anything.
The result

Live at mywaypointnav.com. Passed a full security check and was pitched for pre-seed funding in November 2026. A beta with people leaving incarceration comes next, and VetBridge, the veterans version, is planned.

The Invisible Shelf: MainStreet Local research

Why shoppers can't see what local stores actually have in stock, and how to fix it.
SQUARE + SHOPIFY TESTED
The problem

Local stores track inventory in their point-of-sale systems, but shoppers still can't see what's on the shelf. Each system uses its own data format, and fields with similar names can mean different things. The core question: which quantity can honestly be shown to a shopper as available to buy?

What I did
  • Started MainStreet Local as a product idea, then turned it into a research initiative after realizing the real need was deeper systems. That realization led to Discover Engine.
  • Defined one common stock event that any system can be translated into.
  • Built translators for Square and Shopify data formats and tested them.
The result

All checks passed. Stock changes matched across both systems, and unclear records were marked "unknown" instead of guessed. Next is testing whether timing patterns can work out what each field really means.

SkyHall and the Admin Hub turn dozens of tools into one clear screen.
15 SITES · 1 SCREEN
The problem

Running a company means information spread across dozens of sites, dashboards, inboxes and tools. Keeping it all in your head doesn't scale.
