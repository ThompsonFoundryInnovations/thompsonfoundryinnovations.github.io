It searches. It finds. It collects. Then it makes sense of it.

Discover Engine is TFI's proprietary research and discovery technology. It runs many targeted searches across the web, public records and data sources, gathers what it finds together with your own documents and files, and organizes it all into findings you can trace back to the source, including what conflicts and what is still missing.

Why it matters

Important information is almost never in one place.

It is spread across websites, records, reports and files. People search one site at a time, copy what they find into spreadsheets and decide on whatever turned up first. The source that disagreed gets missed, and when someone asks "where did that come from?", the trail is gone.

Discover Engine changes that. The search becomes thorough and repeatable, every finding keeps its source attached, and the conflicts and gaps are shown instead of smoothed over. The result is an answer that can be checked, shared and trusted.

Hours become minutesDozens of searches run in one pass instead of one browser tab at a time.
Every claim has a receiptEach finding links back to the page, record or file it came from.
Nothing quietly hiddenDisagreements and missing information are shown, not smoothed over.
The same method every timeResults can be compared over time, or between people, because the search is repeatable.
How it works

From a question to everything that can be found about it.

  1. SearchRuns many targeted searches, one per source, plus any you add.
  2. CollectKeeps everything found, plus your files, with where it came from.
  3. ExtractPulls out the facts and ties each one to its source.
  4. CompareLines up the facts and flags conflicts and gaps.
  5. ConfirmA person reviews what matters. AI never gets the final say.
  6. DeliverA report, profile or decision, with its full trail.

Protocol packs

One engine. A different pack for every job.

Think of Discover Engine as the engine block and a protocol pack as the tooling bolted on for one kind of work. The engine stays the same. The pack decides the details.

What it studiesThe kinds of things being researched: a business, a supplier, a vehicle option, a property.
Where it looksWhich sources, files and records count, and how each one is checked.
How it matchesWhat makes two records the same thing, and what makes them different.
How it judgesHow conflicts are handled, what counts as confirmed and which criteria matter.
What it deliversThe report, profile or decision package the person receives.

Decision pack · Working prototype

TFI Titrant

Decision support for evidence-based trade studies. It reads test reports and data, scores each alternative against weighted criteria, shows how sensitive the result is and assembles a decision package a person signs off on.

Titrant was first designed around a defense equipment trade study, where every choice has to be traceable and approved by a person. The same approach is built to work for commercial decisions too: engineering design choices, equipment and vendor selection, and capital planning.

What it proves: the same foundation can be adapted to read documents and test data, weigh alternatives and produce a decision a person signs off on.

Named for the precisely measured solution added until a reaction reaches its endpoint.

MainStreet Local · A local commerce research initiative

The problem is bigger than a search engine.

Solving it starts with understanding it.

The everyday problem

It is probably a mile away. Your phone can’t tell you.

You need a specific thing today. It is probably sitting on a shelf a mile from you. Your phone can show you the store, its reviews and its hours, and still not tell you whether it has the thing. So you order it online, or drive to the big box store.

Shoppers have lived with this for years. In mid-2026, Thompson Foundry Innovations started asking why.

Why it matters

A missed search is a missed sale, and more.

  • Small businesses are most of the economy99.9% of U.S. businesses are small, and they employ 45.9% of private-sector workers (SBA, 2026).
  • Local sales pay for local servicesCalifornia sends 1% of each taxable sale to the city where it happens. In Fullerton, sales tax is 23% of the General Fund (FY2025-26 budget).
  • Search is becoming answersGoogle’s AI summaries reached 1.5 billion monthly users in 2025, and Google’s AI now phones stores to check stock. When a shop’s data is missing, the answer skips it.
  • Being findable paysRestaurants that got listed on a major review platform saw revenue rise about 5% (NBER study).
What we tried, and what we learned

We started building. Then we stepped back.

MainStreet Local began as a plan to connect local businesses and their products with the people searching for them. We explored possible technical approaches, and each one revealed new challenges.

Accurate inventory information is difficult to obtain. Businesses use different systems, and many don’t keep searchable product catalogs. Information changes constantly. Any useful solution also has to respect the time, cost and practical realities of the businesses taking part.

We came to see that this is not simply a software problem. It is an information, business and community challenge, and not a problem any one company can solve alone.

So instead of building around assumptions, we scaled back to study the problem itself. MainStreet Local is now an active research initiative within TFI. We still intend for it to lead this work, and we are asking for help.

Why it is hard

Every fix runs into something real.

  • Inventory data is hard to getGoogle’s free in-store product listings need manufacturer barcodes, a scanner or point-of-sale system, and a verified profile. Only 57% of small businesses use an electronic point-of-sale system.
  • Many goods have no barcodeHandmade, vintage, consignment and prepared goods usually have none, so barcode-based tools leave them out.
  • Every system works differentlySquare, Clover, Lightspeed and Shopify each connect to search in their own way.
  • Information changes constantlyGoogle Maps alone receives about 20 million user contributions a day.
  • Owners are stretchedCost is the top reason small business owners give for not having a website (33%).
  • Nobody is measuring itThere is no official statistic for how many businesses have missing or wrong information online.
Who is involved

Everyone holds one piece.

  • ShoppersSearch and buy

    Find, buy local and report wrong information. They increasingly trust AI answers they cannot check.

  • Local businessesKeep it current

    Keep their details and products up to date, with little time, money or technical help.

  • Point-of-sale companiesSquare, Clover, Shopify

    Connect store inventory to search, but each only for its own customers.

  • Search, maps and AIGoogle, Apple, Yelp, ChatGPT

    Show the answers, blending owner, public and purchased data that often disagree.

  • Business data companiesData Axle, Foursquare

    Keep the business records platforms rely on, checking millions of records by phone and edits.

  • Standards groupsGS1, schema.org

    Publish common formats for products and locations that small shops rarely use.

  • Cities and local partnersCity, chamber, SBDC, libraries

    Organize, fund and train, but no one is assigned this problem.

Measure it, honestly.

TFI’s first step is the Local Discoverability Study: checking how complete and consistent local business information is online, starting in Fullerton.

About

I build systems that make sense of scattered information.

I’m Steven Thompson, founder of Thompson Foundry Innovations.

I started TFI in Fullerton, California, to work on problems where the answers already exist but are scattered across too many places. I research the problem first, then build the system that finds the pieces, fits them together and makes it make sense.

I think of myself as a systems architect. Before TFI, I worked in automotive diagnostics and in warehouse and logistics operations, including on-site at a Google data center. Diagnosing a car and running a warehouse taught me the same lesson: find where the information breaks down, then fix the system, not just the symptom.

I’m neurodivergent, and I build the way I think: in connected parts that fit together. That is why every TFI system is made of components that can be combined and shaped for a new job.

The name comes from the foundry. Raw material goes in, gets shaped under pressure and comes out as something precise and useful. My work starts with fragments of evidence and ends with structure people can rely on.

If you have a problem where the information is scattered, I would like to hear about it.
